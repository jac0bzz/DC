Revision Log: May 2026

1. What is a Cookie?

A cookie is a lightweight text block data file deployed onto your web browser memory or hardware drive by a site system server. Cookies authorize the network layout to register user preferences, languages, and technical presets so you do not have to override configurations on future session returns, enabling responsive and fast interface execution.

2. Cookie Classes We Deploy

On the D&C Standards digital grid, we deploy highly secure data blocks to guarantee the exact fluid representation of our layout components:

Technical & Core Essential Cookies

Critical elements mandatory for core layout navigation and asset component reactions. Without these blocks, interactive functions—such as audio media boards on plant video players, lag-free parallax scrolling, or 3D 60fps lighting animations on product catalog grids—cannot execute.

Performance & Data Analytics Cookies

We deploy anonymous metrics trackers to analyze server loading speeds. This data guides us in identifying which engineering asset catalogs or facility capacity overviews capture higher corporate engagement, allowing us to continuously calibrate our host speed.

3. Third-Party Tracker Cookies

Across specific terminal nodes, we link out to external secure networks to boost engineering B2B responses. Interacting with our floating **WhatsApp Desk** button or exiting towards our institutional **LinkedIn** profile triggers external networks to install individual trackers managed under their unique data terms.

4. User Control & Deactivation

You can reject, restrict, or purge stored tracker cookies at your discretion by modifying your browser settings. Be advised that blocking essential engineering cookies will degrade the structural UI experience, causing dynamic elements to halt responses immediately.

  • Google Chrome: Settings > Privacy and security > Third-party cookies.
  • Brave Browser: Shields > Trackers & cookies blocking toggle.
  • Mozilla Firefox: Settings > Privacy & Security > Cookies and Site Data.
